The map isn’t a square grid. It’s built in four steps, and the pictures below are drawn by the game’s own grid code.

You build on corners

Each grid corner (the dot) owns the quarter of every quad around it. Together those quarters make its cell, the shape you see when hovering.

Stack cells in levels and you get a column of voxels: land at the bottom, houses above. Because every quad still has exactly four corners, the same simple building rules work everywhere, even though no two cells are the same shape.

The one rule in this demo

A tile may only touch itself or its neighbour in this chain:

WaterSandGrassForest

So water can touch sand but never grass, and forest can only grow next to grass.

  1. Start open. Every cell could be any tile (the four small squares).
  2. Pick. Choose the cell with the fewest options left (lowest “entropy”).
  3. Collapse. Decide that cell: a weighted random pick from what’s still allowed.
  4. Propagate. Neighbours drop tiles that can’t touch it, then their neighbours, and so on. Watch the ripple.

Repeat until every cell is decided. Clicking a small square yourself is exactly what building does in the game: you decide one thing, and the rules ripple out.

In the game, the demo’s chain becomes checks between two neighbouring pieces. Left: allowed. Right: ruled out.

A floor shares one window style

Storeys side by side on the same level must use the same windows, so a row of houses reads as one building.

Touching roofs match

Neighbouring roofs share a type (hip, spire or flat terrace) so their ridges meet in one smooth roof.

Doors open onto walkable ground

A door or shop must face open land, and that land can’t be a tree, fountain, market stall or planter.

Woods grow in stands

Pines and oaks never touch directly; a mixed grove sits between them. Clearings are never side by side.

Squares stay roomy

No two fountains or two lamps side by side, and a fountain never sits next to a market stall.

Reeds by the shore, boats offshore

This is a context rule: reeds only grow in water next to land, and boats only float where water surrounds them.

Stable randomness

Every piece has its own seed, which gives each option a fixed “lottery number”. The lowest number that’s still allowed wins, so ruling out some other option never changes the result. Building next door won’t reshuffle your town.

Restyle

Restyle gives the clicked piece a new seed and solves it first, ruling out what it was. Its neighbours re-solve around it, so a whole floor can switch window style together. Everything that changed pops.

Hills and lakes

Land stacks in solid columns, so a taller column is a hill. Each corner (dots) gets one height, computed from every cell that shares it, so slopes are smooth and neighbours never crack apart.

Water pulls its corners down into a basin. The water surface reaches out over the banks, and the rising ground cuts a natural shoreline. A lake above lower ground gets a rim to hold it in. Houses on slopes get stone foundations.

Clicking

A ray finds the triangle under your cursor. Every triangle remembers what clicking it means: build on top, beside it toward a neighbour, or below.

Undo

Each edit keeps a snapshot. Undo swaps it back and rebuilds only the pieces that differ.

Save & share

Every visit starts fresh. Share packs every piece (corner, level, type, colour, seed) into a link to keep or send; tiles are re-solved when it opens.

Look & sound

Low-poly pieces generated in code, turning windmills, bobbing boats, chimney smoke, glints on the sea, birds, day and night, and synthesised sounds.
