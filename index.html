Welcome to Cloudhaven

No goals, no rush. Just build a little town in the sky.

  • Click the clouds to raise an island.
  • Click an island or a roof to build up. Click a wall to build sideways. Overhangs turn into arches and bridges.
  • Land paints meadow, woods, town squares or water. Click the same land again to raise a hill.
  • Restyle (or Alt/⌥-click) swaps a piece for a different version. The pieces around it change to match.
  • Paint (or Shift-click) recolours walls. Erase (or right-click) removes a piece.
  • Drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom.
  • Keys: 1–5 tools, N day/night, Ctrl/Cmd+Z undo (add Shift to redo), ? this help.

Nothing in Cloudhaven is hand-modelled or hand-placed. You decide where things go; a few small algorithms decide what they become.

1 · The irregular grid

The world starts as a hexagon of triangles. Random neighbouring pairs are merged into quads, then every shape is cut into smaller quads (a triangle into 3, a quad into 4), so the whole map is quads. Finally the points are relaxed: each quad is nudged, over many small steps, toward the closest perfect square. The grid looks organic, but every cell still has exactly four corners, which keeps the building rules simple.

You build on the grid's corners. Each corner owns the quarter of every quad that touches it, and together those quarters form its cell (the outline you see when hovering). A cell stacked in levels is a column of voxels: land at the bottom, houses above.

2 · Wave Function Collapse

WFC is a way of filling a space with pieces that fit their neighbours, a bit like solving a sudoku. Every filled voxel starts as a cloud of possibilities and is narrowed down to one tile.

  • Tiles. A storey picks a window style × a facade feature (plain, shutters, flower boxes, balcony, door, shop) × a roof (hip, steep spire, flat terrace). Land picks from its type: meadow (grass, flowers, tree, bush, rocks), woods (pines, oaks, grove, clearing), squares (paving, lamp, bench, planter, fountain, market) or water (open, lilies, reeds, boat, rock).
  • Context. Shape decides what's allowed: roofs only on top floors, doors only on the ground floor facing walkable land, spires only on slim towers, reeds only on a shore, boats only in open water.
  • Rules between neighbours. A floor of a building shares one window style; touching roofs share a type so their ridges meet; no two doors side by side; doors never open onto a tree, fountain or stall; woods grow in pine or oak stands joined by mixed groves; lamps and fountains are spaced out.

The loop: remove every option that breaks a rule with any neighbour (propagation), pick the voxel with the fewest options left and choose one of them, propagate again, repeat until everything is decided.

Only near your click. An edit re-solves just the area around it; the rest of the town stays fixed and acts as a boundary. If that area can't be solved, it grows until it can.

Stable randomness. Each voxel has its own seed, and every option gets a fixed random score from it. The best-scoring option that's still allowed wins, so removing other options never changes the winner. That's why far-away buildings don't reshuffle when you build, and why the same town always looks the same.

Restyle gives a piece a new seed, solves it first while ruling out its current tile, and lets the rest of that floor re-solve around it. Everything that changed pops, so you can see what's connected.

3 · Terrain

Land stacks in solid columns, so a taller column is a hill. Each corner of a cell gets one height, computed from all the cells that share it, so neighbours always meet without cracks. Land averages its neighbours into smooth slopes. Water pulls its corners down into a basin, and the water surface is pushed out over the banks so the slope itself cuts a natural shoreline. Where a lake sits above lower ground, its corners become a rim that holds the water in.

4 · Building the pieces

Each voxel's geometry is generated from its cell and its neighbours. Walls appear wherever the neighbouring quarter is empty. Roofs are a height field that is high in the middle of a building and low at the eaves, so joined houses share one roof. An overhang gets a vaulted underside that dips onto walls beside it, which is where arches and bridges come from. Houses on slopes get stone foundations down to the ground. Windows, doors, trees and lamps are placed with each voxel's seed, so they never jump around.

5 · Everything else

  • Clicking casts a ray into the scene. Every triangle remembers what clicking it means: build on top, build beside it toward a specific neighbour, or build underneath.
  • Undo keeps snapshots of the town; only the voxels that differ are rebuilt.
  • Saving is automatic in your browser. Share compresses the town (corner, level, type, colour and seed per voxel) into the link itself; tiles are re-solved when it opens.
  • Look and feel: flat-shaded low-poly pieces, soft shadows, a sea of instanced clouds, birds, a day/night cycle where windows and lamps light up, springy pop-in animations, and sound effects synthesised on the fly (no audio files).
